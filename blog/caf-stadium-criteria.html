CAF stadium criteria

CAF stadium criteria exist so that African football is played in a safe, modern and professional environment. They decide which competitions a ground may host, and they are the checklist a federation works through before a stadium is approved.

1. Capacity and seating

Minimum spectator capacities differ by competition:

  • Club tournaments: 5,000 to 10,000 spectators
  • National tournaments and the CAF Super Cup: 10,000 to 15,000
  • Africa Cup of Nations: all-seater stadiums of at least 20,000

2. Pitch and surface

  • FIFA-compliant natural grass or synthetic turf
  • Flat, uniform surface with effective drainage
  • 100 to 105 m long, 64 to 68 m wide

3. Lighting and broadcast

  • Floodlighting of at least 1,200 lux; 1,500 lux recommended for AFCON
  • Even light with minimal shadow

4. Safety and emergency systems

  • Security cameras and access control
  • Fire and emergency alarms
  • Emergency exits and signage

5. Media and VIP areas

  • Press seats and media rooms
  • VIP and VVIP areas
  • Broadcaster cabins and camera platforms

6. Technical and support areas

  • Modern dressing rooms, referee rooms and VAR facilities
  • Treatment and rehabilitation rooms
  • Equipment storage

7. Electronic systems

  • LED scoreboards and live scoring
  • Electronic ticketing and access control
  • Broadcast and media integration

8. Comfort and environment

  • Shaded, comfortable seating
  • Ventilation and climate control
  • Clean toilets and food and drink areas

What this means for the gate line

A stadium that meets the criteria can host national and international competitions. Items 4 and 7 are where idCodeX works: electronic ticketing, access control, LED scoreboards and security, integrated so that the whole venue is run from one control room. Hoima City Stadium in Uganda was delivered to these requirements ahead of AFCON 2027.
